Read before the first cut

First furniture build? Start here

These tips are for people building furniture for the first time. Most mistakes on a project like this come from measuring, cut order and glue-ups, not from the saw.

Cut big parts oversize, then trim matching pairs together

Cutting matching parts together is called gang-cutting. Both pieces come out identical, which matters more than hitting the exact decimal.

  1. Rough-cut blanks first. Cut sides to about 27 × 14½ and bases to the plan's 31½ × 14½ blanks. Smaller pieces are easier and safer to handle.
  2. Pair them up. Stack the 2 sides for one unit (1-B1 with 1-B2). Each unit has only 1 base, so stack bases from 2 units that share a sheet (1-A1 with 2-A1). All 4 bases are the same size.
  3. Line up one straight edge. Keep a factory edge on each blank and set the pair flush on it. Hold them with clamps, double-sided tape, or 2 short screws in the waste area, outside the finished part.
  4. Cut in order. Rip to width off the factory edge first, then crosscut to length, square to that edge.
  5. Label right away. Put a tape label on each part and mark its good face before you unstack.

Don't gang-cut the upper shelves. Trim each one to fit its own assembled case. A stacked pair is about 1.4 in thick, which a table saw or track saw handles easily. With a circular saw, use a fine-tooth plywood blade and a straight guide.

Measure and mark

  • Use one tape measure for the whole project. Two tapes can disagree by 1/16.
  • Mark with a sharp pencil or knife and a V-mark (a small arrow), not a thick line.
  • Cut on the waste side of the line. The blade removes about ⅛ in.
  • Where you can, mark parts from the real assembly instead of the tape. Hold a rail against the posts and mark it.

Plywood habits

  • Plywood splinters where the saw teeth exit. On a table saw, put the good face up. With a circular or track saw, put it down.
  • Blue painter's tape over the cut line reduces splintering on the maple face.
  • Support both sides of a cut so the offcut can't drop and tear the last inch.
  • Keep the good face to the outside on sides and up on shelves.

Practice on scrap

  • Make a test cut on an offcut and measure it before cutting a real part. This checks the fence and the blade.
  • Rout the nosing round-over on a spare piece of the 2×4 first.
  • Try the post housings on a scrap of planed 2×2 until a rail fits snugly with hand pressure.
  • Drill the 1 in Forstner hole in scrap and test the dowel fit.

Dry-fit, then glue

  • Assemble each unit with clamps and no glue first. Fix gaps before glue is involved.
  • Lay out every clamp you need before opening the glue. Most wood glue gives you 5–10 minutes.
  • Check square by measuring both diagonals. When they match, the case is square.
  • Wipe squeeze-out with a damp rag. Dried glue blocks the finish.

Screws

  • Drill a pilot hole for every screw. Without one, screws split solid wood and plywood edges.
  • Use a countersink so heads sit flush.
  • Set the drill clutch low so you don't strip the hole or sink the head too far.
  • Glue makes the joint. The screws only hold parts in place while it dries.

Work safely

  • Wear eye and hearing protection, and a dust mask when sanding or routing.
  • Use a push stick for any rip narrower than about 6 in, including the nosing strips.
  • Never cut freehand on a table saw. Every cut runs against the fence or a sled.
  • Don't plane pieces shorter than your planer's minimum length. Plane the 2×2s while they're long.

2. Part labels

2-B1T1-K2

Write build number - part letter + number on painter's tape with a Sharpie as soon as a part is cut. Shelves are builds 1–4. Totes are T1 and T2.

Left and right are as seen from the front of the finished shelf. Put the tape on a face that will be hidden (inside of a side, underside of a shelf, back of a rail), and draw an arrow on it pointing to the front or top edge. Letters I, O and Q are skipped so they can't be read as 1 or 0. Keep each build's parts in its own stack or bin.

3. Build tracker

Tap a label once the part is cut and taped. Everyone with the page open sees it update, with who cut it and when. Tap again to undo.
